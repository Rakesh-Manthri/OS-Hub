Classic Synchronization

The Readers–Writers Problem

Multiple reader processes may access a shared resource at the same time, but a writer needs exclusive access. The synchronization challenge is to protect the critical section while deciding which waiting process gets in next — and to avoid starving either side.

Readers First

Reader preference

A reader may enter whenever no writer is active, even if writers are already waiting. Writers wait until the last active reader leaves. Continuous reader arrivals can keep the writer waiting forever.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: High reader concurrency and simple code.
  • Cons: Writer starvation under a reader-heavy load.

Writers First

Writer preference

Once a writer is waiting, new readers are held back. Active readers finish, the writer runs exclusively, and queued writers take priority. A steady stream of writers can starve readers.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Writers make progress and data stays fresh.
  • Cons: Reader starvation under a writer-heavy load.

Fair / FIFO

FIFO

Processes are granted access in arrival order. A writer at the front waits for active readers to drain; a reader at the front enters immediately. Consecutive readers may still read together. Neither side can be overtaken indefinitely.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Bounded waiting and no starvation.
  • Cons: Lower peak throughput than reader preference.

The resource is a database, file, or shared record. Readers only observe state, so concurrent reads are safe. Writers change state, so no other reader or writer may run at the same time.

Metrics
Wait = grant tick − arrival tick Throughput = completed ÷ ticks Reader concurrency = active readers Starvation = a later arrival completes first

The simulator reports overtaking directly: a process is flagged when a process that arrived later reaches the done state while it is still waiting. This makes starvation visible even when the starved process eventually runs.

Why Starvation Happens
Reader stream
New readers keep joining while the writer waits. Writers preference blocks those readers; readers preference does not.
Writer stream
Queued writers keep replacing the active writer. Readers preference lets readers through; writers preference keeps admitting writers.
FIFO fix
Grant service in arrival order. Once a process is at the front, later arrivals cannot pass it, so waiting is bounded.
