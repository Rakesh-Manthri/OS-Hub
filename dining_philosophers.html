Classic Synchronization

The Dining Philosophers Problem

Five philosophers sit around a circular table with one fork between each pair. A philosopher needs both adjacent forks to eat, but may hold only one at a time while waiting for the other. If everyone takes the left fork simultaneously, every philosopher waits forever for the right fork: a classic circular-wait deadlock.

Naive

Left then right

Each philosopher picks up the left fork first and then the right fork. This satisfies all four Coffman conditions and can deadlock when every philosopher holds one fork.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Simple and intuitive.
  • Cons: Not deadlock-free under a synchronized start.

Ordered

Resource hierarchy

Always acquire the lower-numbered fork before the higher-numbered fork. The global order removes circular wait, so a cycle of philosophers waiting on each other cannot form.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Deadlock-free without a central arbiter.
  • Cons: Requires a consistent resource ordering.

Waiter

Arbitrator

A waiter allows at most N − 1 philosophers to compete at the table. With at least one philosopher outside, at least one fork remains available, so progress is always possible.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Simple centralized deadlock prevention.
  • Cons: The waiter is a shared bottleneck.
Coffman Conditions
Mutual exclusionOne owner per fork
Hold and waitHold one fork, request the next
No preemptionForks are released voluntarily
Circular waitP0 → P1 → … → P0

All four conditions must hold for deadlock. Ordered acquisition removes circular wait; the waiter removes the possibility that every philosopher holds a fork.

Metrics
Meals served = completed meals Wait = ticks between hungry and eating Throughput = meals ÷ ticks Safe ⇔ no circular wait

The simulator counts one fork acquisition per philosopher per tick. This makes the hold-and-wait window visible and lets the naive strategy deadlock deterministically from a synchronized hungry start.
