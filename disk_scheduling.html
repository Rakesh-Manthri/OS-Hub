Disk Scheduling

Disk Scheduling Algorithms Guide

Disk scheduling decides the order in which pending I/O requests are serviced. Moving the head fewer cylinders usually means lower seek time, so every algorithm here is judged by its total head movement.

FCFS

First Come, First Served

Serve requests in the exact order they arrive. The head may sweep back and forth across the disk.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Fair, simple, and starvation-free.
  • Cons: Often produces very large total movement.

SSTF

Shortest Seek First

Always serve the pending request closest to the current head position. This is greedy nearest-neighbour scheduling.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Usually much better than FCFS.
  • Cons: Can starve far-away requests; not globally optimal.

SCAN

Elevator Algorithm

Move in one direction serving requests, reach the disk edge, then reverse and serve the remaining requests.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Bounded waiting and steady throughput.
  • Cons: Travel to the edge may be wasted if no request lies there.

C-SCAN

Circular SCAN

Sweep in one direction to the disk edge, jump back to the opposite edge, and continue sweeping in the same direction.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: More uniform wait times than SCAN.
  • Cons: The return jump adds seek distance.

LOOK

Look Ahead SCAN

Like SCAN, but reverse as soon as no more requests lie ahead instead of travelling all the way to the disk edge.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Removes unnecessary travel to empty edges.
  • Cons: Requests near the edge can still wait longer.

C-LOOK

Circular LOOK

Serve requests in one direction, then jump directly to the farthest request on the opposite side and continue in the same direction.

Trade-offs:
  • Pros: Avoids travel to empty disk edges and keeps circular fairness.
  • Cons: The wrap-around jump can still be expensive.
Seek Metrics
Seek distance = |current track − next track| Total movement = Σ seek distances Average seek = Total movement ÷ requests served

This simulator counts the circular return jump in C-SCAN and C-LOOK, which is why those totals can exceed their non-circular counterparts.
